# Sky365 Two Growth Paths — Strategy and Execution Roadmap

Date: 2026-07-27  
Owner: Sky365 leadership  
Scope: Sky365 AI Research & Discovery Platform and Sky365 Enterprise Business Platform  
Repository role: primary home of Path One

![Sky365 two AI-native growth engines](assets/sky365-two-ai-native-growth-engines-v2.png)

## Purpose

Sky365 has two independent, AI-native commercial paths. They share knowledge,
selected technology, and future connectors, but they do not depend on each
other for product identity or market entry.

The portfolio priority is the **highest sustainable expected income**. This
means protecting proven revenue while building bounded new opportunities that
can earn customers, subscriptions, partnerships, or investment.

## Evidence language

Every agent and roadmap report must use these statuses consistently:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| IDEA | Strategic possibility; no validated demand or implementation proof |
| DISCOVERY | Problem, customer, market, and implementation options are being investigated |
| PROTOTYPE | Demonstration exists; it is not production or commercial proof |
| WORKING | Code-level or controlled runtime evidence exists |
| LIVE | The intended deployed environment and real workflow were verified |
| PAID | Revenue, paid pilot, signed delivery, or active paid subscription is evidenced |
| PARTNERED | A real external partnership is agreed and evidenced |

Listing a capability in this document does not imply that it is working, live,
paid, or partnered.

# Path One — Sky365 AI Research & Discovery Platform

## Product promise

Help people and organizations search, understand, build, and apply artificial
intelligence through trusted discovery, useful tools, practical knowledge, and
connected automation.

Working public name: **Sky365 AI Search & Discovery Platform**.

The final name remains a market decision. The product must be immediately
understandable as an AI search platform while remaining broad enough for
research, tools, knowledge, builders, cybersecurity, media, robotics, and
partnerships.

AnythingLLM is an implementation foundation and control plane. It is not the
public product boundary or the complete Sky365 value proposition.

## Target audiences

- Individuals seeking AI search, answers, and practical tools.
- Researchers, learners, prompt practitioners, and creators.
- Developers and agent or automation builders.
- Small businesses buying focused AI tools.
- Enterprises connecting AI to systems they already own.
- Technology partners, model providers, distributors, and investors.

## Product portfolio

### Search and knowledge

- AI Search and trusted answer experience.
- Research workflows and source-grounded discovery.
- RAG workspaces and knowledge retrieval.
- NLP Platform services.
- Documentation portal and documentation intelligence.
- Prompting library, education, and reusable prompt systems.
- Knowledge publishing as a public contribution.

### Builders and digital workforce

- Agent Building.
- Workers and task-specific AI workers.
- Worker Builder.
- MCP tools and tool marketplace direction.
- Automation, scheduled work, and multi-step flows.
- APIs, SDKs, and embeddable capabilities.

### Media and frontier projects

- Video Generator.
- Voice projects and conversational interfaces.
- Image and multimodal generation.
- Robotics and physical automation research.
- Open-source projects and reusable components.

### Trust and cybersecurity

- Cybersecurity research and focused security tools.
- NTM SIEM path.
- AI safety, privacy, evaluation, and operational observability.
- Evidence-backed reviews and technical opinions.

### Growth and distribution

- B2C subscriptions.
- Separately priced small AI tools.
- B2B APIs and connected tools.
- White-label and embedded experiences.
- Open-source-led adoption.
- Partnerships, sponsorships, grants, and strategic investment.

## Commercial model

Possible revenue streams must be tested independently:

1. Free search with paid usage or capability limits.
2. Individual subscription.
3. Team and business subscription.
4. Per-tool subscription or one-time paid utility.
5. Token or consumption-based usage.
6. B2B API and connector usage.
7. White-label licensing.
8. Implementation, integration, and support services.
9. Sponsored research or strategic partnerships.
10. Grants and investment tied to a measurable platform thesis.

Do not assume one pricing model fits every tool.

## Landing-page contract

The first screen must communicate:

- this is an AI search and discovery platform;
- the visitor can search or try a tool now;
- subscriptions and business use are available;
- investors and strategic partners have a direct path to engage.

Primary calls to action:

- **Search with Sky365**
- **Explore AI Tools**
- **Partner or Invest**

The funding path must lead to an investor or partnership brief containing the
problem, platform thesis, differentiated assets, traction evidence, business
model, funding request, use of funds, milestones, and contact route.

## Execution stages

### A1 — Position and validate

- Test the public name and one-sentence promise.
- Identify the first search audience and top three use cases.
- Define trust, citation, privacy, and content-source rules.
- Produce the landing page, investor path, and measurable conversion events.

Exit gate: users understand the offer; at least one target segment and one
monetization hypothesis are selected.

### A2 — Launch the search wedge

- Deliver the smallest credible AI search and trusted answer journey.
- Connect the minimum required providers, retrieval, and source display.
- Add onboarding, identity, feedback, and operational measurement.
- Publish clear evidence boundaries and acceptable-use terms.

Exit gate: a real user can complete the intended search journey and the team
can measure activation, answer quality, cost, and retention.

### A3 — Monetize

- Add plans, entitlements or tokens, usage accounting, and payment paths.
- Select a small portfolio of separately understandable AI tools.
- Price each offer against its own customer value and delivery cost.
- Establish support and incident ownership.

Exit gate: paid conversion or a paid pilot is evidenced; unit economics can be
estimated from real usage.

### A4 — Build the ecosystem

- Release Agent Building, Workers, Worker Builder, and automation in bounded
  vertical slices.
- Expand documentation, prompting, NLP, RAG, video, voice, cybersecurity,
  NTM SIEM, robotics, and open source according to demand evidence.
- Establish partner onboarding and distribution agreements.

Exit gate: each active product line has a customer, owner, revenue model,
smallest sellable release, evidence, and next gate.

### A5 — Connected B2B platform

- Offer APIs, MCP tools, embedded experiences, and white-label packages.
- Validate the Universal Connector with a real customer or partner.
- Connect to Sky365 Enterprise or third-party systems without requiring ERP
  replacement.

Exit gate: a connected workflow is live with explicit permissions, audit,
commercial ownership, support, and measurable customer value.

## Path One metrics

- Search activation and successful task completion.
- Answer-quality and source-trust measures.
- Return usage and retention.
- Free-to-paid conversion.
- Revenue and gross margin per product or tool.
- Inference, retrieval, storage, and support cost per active customer.
- Partner-sourced opportunities and conversion.
- Time from idea to first sellable release.
- Paid B2B usage and connector adoption.

# Path Two — Sky365 Enterprise Business Platform

## Product promise

Deliver AI-native, metadata-driven business systems that run real operations,
improve decisions, and generate durable customer and service revenue.

Enterprise is not the non-AI side of Sky365. It contains AI agents, copilots,
automation, knowledge, analytics, and intelligent engines inside governed
business workflows.

## Target audiences

- Existing Sky365 customers and active delivery commitments.
- Businesses needing ERP, CRM, WMS, finance, HR, CMS, or workflow solutions.
- Vertical markets served by products such as Salogi.
- Organizations modernizing legacy or metadata-driven applications.
- Partners needing implementation, extension, and managed services.

## Enterprise engine portfolio

- Dynamic Engine and metadata-driven application runtime.
- Workflow Engine and approvals.
- Agentic AI Engine, agents, copilots, and governed tool execution.
- ERP, CRM, WMS, Finance, HR, and CMS capabilities.
- Analytics, dashboards, reporting, and decision support.
- Identity, permissions, tokens, audit, and governance.
- Integration, APIs, MCP, and automation.
- Vertical products, customer-specific extensions, and service delivery.

## Commercial model

1. Paid implementation and customer delivery.
2. Recurring subscription or licensing.
3. Vertical-product subscriptions.
4. Support, maintenance, hosting, and managed services.
5. Integration, migration, and customization.
6. Premium AI agents, analytics, and automation.
7. Reusable engines or modules licensed to partners.
8. Connected AI tools sold without mandatory ERP replacement.

## Execution stages

### E1 — Protect income and delivery

- Prioritize contracted work, live customer blockers, billing, and operational
  completion.
- Keep scope, acceptance evidence, support, and payment milestones explicit.
- Avoid speculative platform work that delays customer value.

Exit gate: delivery evidence and commercial milestone are accepted.

### E2 — Productize repeated value

- Identify repeated customer work that can become metadata, an engine, module,
  template, connector, or vertical product.
- Separate reusable IP from customer-specific data and rules.
- Measure reuse by reduced delivery time or increased price and margin.

Exit gate: the reusable capability succeeds in a second real use case or
materially reduces delivery cost.

### E3 — Expand AI-native operations

- Add governed agents and automation to high-value workflows.
- Preserve authorization, approvals, audit, and transactional truth.
- Price AI capability by business outcome, avoided cost, or premium service.

Exit gate: the workflow is live, governed, measurable, and commercially owned.

### E4 — Scale vertical products and partnerships

- Turn proven delivery patterns into repeatable industry offers.
- Establish onboarding, implementation templates, support tiers, and partner
  channels.
- Reuse suitable AI Search & Discovery capabilities through contracts rather
  than repository coupling.

Exit gate: repeatable sale and delivery are evidenced across multiple
customers or a signed distribution partner.

## Path Two metrics

- Contracted and collected revenue.
- Recurring revenue and renewal.
- Gross margin and delivery utilization.
- Milestone acceptance and time to value.
- Support burden and incident rate.
- Reuse across customers and reduced implementation time.
- Paid adoption of AI agents, automation, and analytics.
- Expansion revenue per customer.
- Vertical-product pipeline and win rate.

# Shared bridge — Sky365 Universal Connector

## Purpose

Allow a Sky365 AI subscriber to apply AI tools to the business system they
already own, including Sky365 Enterprise or a third-party ERP, CRM, database,
productivity suite, or custom application.

## Possible contract

- Identity federation and tenant mapping.
- Entitlements or token exchange.
- API and MCP capability discovery.
- Read, propose, approve, and execute permission levels.
- Automation triggers and event exchange.
- Audit, usage accounting, observability, and billing.

## Non-negotiable boundary

The AI platform must not bypass the target system's permissions, business
rules, approvals, or audit. The connector is a commercial product only when a
specific customer outcome and accountable owner exist.

# Cross-path sourcing decision

When one path needs a capability associated with the other, evaluate:

| Option | Choose when |
|---|---|
| Reuse directly | Boundaries, ownership, release cadence, and support are already compatible |
| Extract as a shared service or library | Reuse is material and a stable contract can be owned |
| Connect through API or MCP | Independence, permissions, or different release cadences matter |
| Buy or partner | Time to revenue and total cost beat internal construction |
| Use open source | License, security, maintenance, and differentiation are acceptable |
| Build separately | The capability is strategically differentiating and coupling cost is high |
| Defer | No customer, revenue, risk-reduction, or learning case justifies work now |

## Decision score

Score every material initiative from 1 to 5:

- expected revenue;
- probability of success;
- time to first revenue;
- recurring value;
- strategic ownership and reuse;
- delivery and operating cost;
- legal, security, and reputation risk.

Document the evidence behind the score. A confident-looking number without
evidence is not a decision.

# Portfolio operating rhythm

For each active initiative, maintain:

- customer and pain;
- product offer;
- current evidence status;
- smallest sellable release;
- revenue model and expected margin;
- accountable owner;
- next test and decision gate;
- stop, continue, partner, or scale decision.

Review the two paths together for capital allocation, but execute them through
separate backlogs. Proven Enterprise income is protected; Path One receives
bounded investment based on search adoption, paid tools, partnerships, or
credible funding evidence.
